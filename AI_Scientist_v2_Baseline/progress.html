Each experiment trains a candidate train.py for a fixed 300 s of wall clock, then scores it with val_bpb. Chart conventions follow analysis.ipynb from the reference repo: grey for discarded runs, green for kept ones, and a step line tracking the running best.

Why the bigger model won

The depth sweep with everything else held fixed. Quality is single-peaked at 85.9M parameters, but GPU utilisation climbs the whole way. That is why a Chinchilla-style tokens-per-parameter argument fails here — under a wall-clock budget, a larger model is handed more actual FLOPs rather than spending a fixed number differently.

What has been established

  • Noise floor 0.000148 — standard deviation of three byte-identical runs. Anything under ~0.0003 is unresolvable at n=1; a 0.001 effect is ~7σ.
  • Batch 218 is optimal at both model sizes, bracketed on both sides. The prediction that critical batch size would grow with the model was wrong: at fixed wall clock, doubling the batch simply halves the step count.
  • Capacity is single-peaked at DEPTH 10 — 26.3M and 135.3M both lose to 85.9M.
  • Adam-group LRs were under-tuned; optimizer geometry was not. EMBEDDING_LR 0.6→0.8 won 0.0018 (~12σ) and 1.0 gave it back, so the optimum sits near 0.8 — while MATRIX_LR, batch size and WARMDOWN_RATIO were all already correct.
