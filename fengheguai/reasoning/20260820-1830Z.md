## t0036 · explore · discard · val_bpb 0.977341

- **best after:** 0.973097

**Hypothesis (pre-registered).** The output softcap may be limiting the metric directly rather than only stabilising training. The head computes logits, casts to float32, then applies 15*tanh(logits/15), which asymptotically bounds every logit to plus or minus 15 and, more importantly, compresses differences once magnitudes approach that scale. val_bpb is a cross-entropy quantity, so it is exactly the size of the gap between the correct logit and its competitors that sets the achievable loss on confident predictions, and a model at 0.973 bpb makes many highly confident predictions on a small 8192-token vocabulary. If the cap binds on those, it puts a floor under the loss that no amount of better representation can pass. Doubling the cap to 30 keeps the same stabilising shape near the origin, since tanh is linear there, while relaxing the ceiling where it binds, and should lower val_bpb.

**Predicted effect.** Expected between a 0.002 decrease and a 0.001 increase versus champion t0034 at 0.973097, with step count unchanged since tanh on the logits is a trivial elementwise cost either way. A decrease would mean the cap was binding on confident predictions and would justify testing a still looser cap or removing it; an increase would mean the compression is doing real optimisation work, most plausibly by bounding the gradient into lm_head early in training when logits are poorly calibrated.

**Stated risk.** Softcapping is usually introduced for stability rather than for quality, so relaxing it can surface the instability it was added to prevent, which here would appear as a much worse loss or the FAIL exit that triggers when train_loss exceeds 100 or goes NaN rather than as a small regression.
