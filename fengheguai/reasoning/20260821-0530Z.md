**Hypothesis (pre-registered).** The MLP nonlinearity is the last untouched component of the transformer block. Every other part has been measured: attention span and pattern, head width and count, value embeddings and their gate, the residual and skip scalars, depth, width and the MLP expansion ratio. ## t0096 · explore · keep · val_bpb 0.967286

- **best after:** 0.967286  ← new champion

**Hypothesis (pre-registered).** Weight averaging was dismissed early in this campaign on a premise that has since become false. The argument then was that the schedule anneals the rate to zero, so the final weights are already settled and Polyak averaging has nothing to denoise. That stopped being true at t0048, which introduced FINAL_LR_FRAC of 0.1 and was promoted: the model now finishes training at a tenth of peak rate, still taking meaningful steps, so its final parameters are a single noisy draw from a distribution rather than a converged point. Averaging the tail of the trajectory should land closer to the centre of that distribution than any individual endpoint. The cost is small and can be checked rather than assumed: the averaged copy is about 96M parameters, so each update moves roughly 1GB of memory traffic against a 105ms step, which is well under one percent.

**Change.** Polyak weight averaging added to champion t0089. Two constants are introduced, EMA_START at 0.7 and EMA_DECAY at 0.99. After each optimizer step past 70 percent of the time budget, an fp32 running average of every parameter is updated by lerp, and immediately before the single locked evaluate_bpb call the averaged weights are copied into the model. With about 2875 steps and 30 percent of the budget in the averaging phase, the window covers roughly 860 steps at a decay of 0.99, an effective horizon near 100 steps. Nothing else changes: the averaging is computed purely from training parameters, touches no validation data, and the evaluation remains exactly one ordinary call to the immutable evaluator, which scores whatever weights the model holds at that moment.

**Predicted effect.** Expected between a 0.002 decrease and a 0.001 increase versus champion t0089 at 0.968389. The averaging happens inside the timed region so its cost is charged honestly to the 300 seconds, and num_steps should fall by well under one percent from 2875 if the traffic estimate is right; a larger fall would mean the estimate was wrong, which is a live possibility given that t0092 slowed by 100ms per step for reasons my memory-traffic arithmetic did not predict.

**Stated risk.** The decay and the start point are guesses rather than measurements, and they interact: a 0.99 decay over an 860-step window averages over roughly the last 100 steps, which may be too short to denoise or long enough to drag in weights from a materially higher learning rate. The deeper risk is that averaging fights the annealing schedule, since the whole point of a decaying rate is to settle the model, and if the last 30 percent is already settled then the average is over a stationary point and this measures nothing. If it regresses I will not tune the two constants; the honest reading would be that the schedule already does this job.
