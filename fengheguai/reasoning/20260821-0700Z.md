## t0104 · explore · discard · val_bpb 0.969680

- **best after:** 0.967286

**Hypothesis (pre-registered).** t0103 tested Lookahead on every parameter and lost 0.007204, and its risk note named the likely cause in advance: Muon maintains a momentum buffer and a NorMuon per-row variance estimate keyed to the parameters it is updating, and Lookahead rewrites those parameters without touching either, so for five steps after each sync the orthogonalised update is correcting a trajectory that no longer exists. ## t0105 · recombine · discard · val_bpb 0.968249

- **best after:** 0.967286

**Hypothesis (pre-registered).** Logit magnitude is the most sensitive quantity this campaign has found, and only one instrument controls it. The output softcap is the single inherited default that proved suboptimal, and its curve is steep in both directions: 7 costs 0.005206, 10 was the optimum at the old batch, 13 is the optimum now, 30 costs 0.004637. Both failures converge on the same mechanism, damping the gradient into lm_head, and t0039 showed that a uniform learning-rate reduction cannot substitute for it because the cap acts selectively on tokens whose logits have grown large. A z-loss is the other selective instrument for the same quantity: it penalises the log-partition, so it pushes back on exactly the examples whose logits are collectively large, and it does so through the loss rather than by clipping the function. If softcapping this model is worth several thousandths of a bpb, a second, smoother control on the same quantity is the most promising remaining lever.

**Change.** Auxiliary z-loss added to champion t0096. GPT.forward gains a z_loss keyword defaulting to 0.0; when positive it adds the coefficient times the mean squared log-sum-exp of the logits over non-ignored targets. The training loop passes Z_LOSS of 1e-4. The default of 0.0 is what makes this safe for the contract: prepare.evaluate_bpb calls the model as model(x, y, reduction=none) and cannot pass the keyword, so the locked metric is computed from cross-entropy alone and the penalty exists only in the training objective. The output softcap of 13 is retained rather than replaced, since the two act differently, one clipping the function and one shaping the loss. Everything else matches the champion, including the endpoint EMA, the linear anneal, depth 12, the SSL pattern with a 256-token short span, document masking, batch 2**17 and WEIGHT_DECAY 0.146.

**Predicted effect.** Expected between a 0.002 decrease and a 0.002 increase versus champion t0096 at 0.967286, with the step count falling by perhaps one percent from the extra logsumexp over a vocabulary of 8192 in fp32. A coefficient of 1e-4 is the conventional starting point and is deliberately small: the point of this node is to establish the sign of the effect on an axis known to be steep, not to tune the coefficient.

**Stated risk.** The softcap already bounds every logit at 13, so the log-partition cannot grow without limit and the z-loss may be pushing against a constraint that is no longer binding, which would make this a null. If it instead regresses, the likely reading is that the two controls fight: tanh saturation shapes large logits selectively while the z-loss penalises their aggregate, so a model already capped may be paying twice for the same behaviour. There is also a real chance the coefficient is simply mis-scaled for a vocabulary this small, since 1e-4 is conventional at vocabularies in the tens of thousands where the log-partition is correspondingly larger.

## t0106 · explore · failed · val_bpb —

- **best after:** 0.967286

**Hypothesis (pre-registered).** The training context length has never been varied, and document masking has made most of it unused. Attention now costs about 62.9M of the roughly 314M per-token FLOPs, and 50.3M of that sits in the four full-context layers spanning 2048 tokens; the other eight layers see only 256. Since t0010 those full-context layers cannot read across a document boundary anyway, so the span they actually exploit is the length of the enclosing document rather than the row. Halving the training context to 1024 halves the cost of those four layers, removing about 25M FLOPs per token or 8 percent of the total, which should convert into roughly 6 to 8 percent more optimizer steps. Evaluation is untouched: prepare.evaluate_bpb builds its own loader at MAX_SEQ_LEN and the model handles a longer input at eval because the rotary table is precomputed for ten times the configured length.

**Change.** Training context reduced on champion t0096: a new TRAIN_SEQ_LEN of 1024 replaces MAX_SEQ_LEN in both the model config and the training dataloader, and DEVICE_BATCH_SIZE doubles from 64 to 128 so tokens per forward-backward stays at exactly 2**17 and gradient accumulation stays at 1. The window sizes derive from config.sequence_len, so the four full-context layers now span 1024 and the eight short layers keep their 256-token span. Evaluation is unchanged and remains exact: prepare.evaluate_bpb divides EVAL_TOKENS by batch times MAX_SEQ_LEN, which at 128 by 2048 gives exactly 80 steps over the same 20,971,520 validation tokens as every previous trial. Everything else matches the champion, including the endpoint EMA, the linear anneal, document masking, depth 12, batch 2**17 and WEIGHT_DECAY 0.146.

**Predicted effect.** Expected between a 0.002 decrease and a 0.003 increase versus champion t0096 at 0.967286, with the step count rising to roughly 3050 from 2870. The case for a gain is that the freed FLOPs are real and the context they buy is largely unused after masking; the case against is that documents longer than 1024 tokens do exist and the full-context layers currently serve them, so this trades a genuine capability for throughput.

**Stated risk.** This introduces a train-evaluate mismatch, which no previous node has done: the model trains on 1024-token rows and is scored on 2048-token rows. That is safe mechanically, since rotary is precomputed for ten times the configured length and the window sizes cap attention below the input length, but it means the four global layers are asked at evaluation to attend over a span twice anything they saw in training. If the result regresses sharply that mismatch is the likeliest cause rather than the lost context itself, and the clean way to separate them would be a node that also evaluates at 1024, which the locked evaluator does not permit.
