## t0362 · recombine · discard · val_bpb 0.982245

- **best after:** 0.966202

**Hypothesis (pre-registered).** t0361 decayed all three embedding tables at the matrix rate and lost 0.013321 of mechanism after subtracting a 2.32 percent throughput cost, which is by far the largest regression this line has produced. I pre-registered that if it destabilised the unembedding would be the first suspect, because its weight decay coefficient of 0.97163 looked alarming next to the others, and that the follow-up would be to decay the token and value embeddings only. That reasoning was wrong and I am departing from it deliberately rather than quietly. The coefficient was never the issue; all four families were decaying at the identical rate of 0.00238, which is the whole point of deriving it from the product, and I said in the same record that the number looks wrong and is not. The discriminator is gradient density. wte and the value embeddings are lookup tables: a row receives a gradient only on steps where its token actually appears in the batch, while p.mul_(1 - lr*wd) shrinks every row on every step, so rare tokens are pulled toward zero with nothing pushing back and their embeddings are destroyed over 2778 steps. lm_head is dense, since every row participates in the softmax at every position, so it is the one table whose decay is balanced by a gradient on every step. This predicts that the harm in t0361 came from the two sparse tables and that decaying the unembedding alone is safe.

**Change.** One regularisation change on champion t0353, narrower than t0361. A constant UNEMBEDDING_DECAY_RATE of 0.00238, equal to MATRIX_LR times WEIGHT_DECAY, is introduced, and only the lm_head AdamW group receives weight_decay equal to that rate divided by its own learning rate, giving wd 0.97163 at lr 0.00245 so the realised product is 0.00238. The token embedding and value embedding groups are returned to weight_decay 0.0, as are the two scalar groups, and the Muon groups are untouched at 0.146. Compared with t0361 this decays 33.5M parameters instead of about 100M, so the optimizer touches a third as much memory per step. No architecture change.

**Predicted effect.** Expected between 0.9655 and 0.9670 against champion 0.966202, with steps recovering most of the way from 2714 back toward 2778, since the per-step multiply now covers one table rather than three; I expect roughly a 0.8 percent cost rather than 2.32 percent. The sharp prediction is about the split: if gradient density is the right discriminator, this should sit near or below the champion rather than anywhere near t0361 at 0.981003. A result close to the champion confirms the sparse tables caused the damage and leaves open whether unembedding decay is mildly good or neutral. A result far above the champion would falsify the density story and say that this recipe simply does not want any embedding-family decay at all, which closes the axis.

**Stated risk.** The main risk is that the effect is small either way and the trial only rules out a hypothesis rather than gaining ground, which I accept because t0361 was expensive enough that the axis deserves one properly targeted follow-up before being abandoned. A subtler risk is that lm_head decay interacts with the output softcap at 13, since shrinking the unembedding shrinks logits and the softcap is tuned to the current logit scale, so a mild regression might reflect a softcap that is now mistuned rather than decay being wrong; the signature would be a regression of a few tenths of a percent with steps intact, and the disambiguating follow-up would be the same decay with the softcap re-tuned, not a decay rate scan.

## t0363 · explore · discard · val_bpb 0.967716

- **best after:** 0.966202

**Hypothesis (pre-registered).** First, a correction that this trial does not depend on but the record should carry. t0362 decayed only the unembedding and scored 0.982245, a mechanism cost of 0.015004, worse than t0361 which decayed all three tables at 0.013321. I predicted 0.9655 to 0.9670 on a gradient-density argument, so that argument is falsified: the unembedding is precisely the table that cannot tolerate decay, which is what my original pre-registration guessed on grounds I dismissed as an unsound magnitude heuristic. The real discriminator is initialisation scale, not gradient density. lm_head is initialised at std 0.001 against wte at 1.0, so it starts a thousand times smaller and has to grow during training, and a decay of 0.00238 per step prevents that growth and pins the logits near zero. Density was irrelevant. Second, the actual subject of this trial, found by auditing init_weights during a training window. The champion zeroes ve_gate so that 2*sigmoid(0) equals exactly 1.0 and the gate starts neutral, but attn_gate, added in t0353 and the entire mechanism of the current champion, was never added to that loop and keeps nn.Linear's default U(+-1/sqrt(128)). With 128 post-norm inputs of unit RMS the pre-activation standard deviation is 0.577, so the per-head gates start scattered from about 0.48 to 1.52 rather than at 1.0. Every layer therefore begins by applying a random per-head rescaling of its attention output, and part of a 300 second budget is spent undoing it. I claimed the opposite in the record of t0353, t0354 and t0358, describing the gate as near-identity at initialisation; that claim was design intent that I never checked against the code and then copied forward.

**Change.** One initialisation change on champion t0353, with no change to any forward path. In init_weights, the loop that zeroes gate weights now also zeroes block.attn.attn_gate.weight for every block, alongside the existing ve_gate zeroing which was already conditional on that gate existing. The attention gate therefore starts at exactly 2*sigmoid(0) = 1.0 for every head in every layer, which is the convention the file already states in its own comment and applies to the value-embedding gate. Nothing else moves: the gate keeps its position before c_proj, its head basis, its n_head granularity, its 2*sigmoid form and its presence on all twelve layers, all of which t0355 through t0358 established. No parameters are added or removed, so the optimizer groups and the parameter-count assertion are unchanged.

**Predicted effect.** Expected between 0.9645 and 0.9662 against champion 0.966202, with steps within noise of 2778 since initialisation costs nothing at runtime and no kernel work changes. This is the cleanest possible read: zero throughput term, one line, and a mechanism whose value is already measured at 0.0031. A decrease says the champion has been paying to undo its own random initialisation and the gate is worth more than measured. A null says the model absorbs the random start within the first few hundred steps and the initialisation never mattered, which would be worth knowing before I trust any other near-identity claim in this file.

**Stated risk.** The most likely outcome is a small improvement or a null rather than anything dramatic, since the model has 2778 steps to correct a starting offset and Muon updates these weights aggressively. A subtler risk is that the random initialisation was accidentally useful, breaking symmetry between heads that would otherwise receive identical gradients through the gate; if zero-init regresses, that is the explanation, and the disambiguating follow-up would be a small non-zero symmetric init such as std 0.01 rather than a return to the default. I also want to flag that the queued next candidate is stronger than usual: decomposing t0361 against t0362 implies that decaying the token and value embeddings alone, without the unembedding, is worth about -0.0017, which would beat the champion. That estimate assumes additivity across two runs far outside the champion regime, so it needs its own trial rather than being folded in here.
