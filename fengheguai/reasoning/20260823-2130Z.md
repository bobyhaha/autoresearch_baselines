**Hypothesis (pre-registered).** Two trials have now bracketed the MLP token-identity mechanism precisely. t0372 gave the MLP its own per-layer tables at full width: mechanism -0.002296, real and large, but 50.3M parameters cost 4.71 percent of steps for a tax of +0.002826, so it lost by 0.000530. t0373 tried to get the same mechanism for free by letting the MLP read the value embedding the layer already gathers, adding 1536 parameters: mechanism +0.000707, actively worse than no injection at all. That is the signature I pre-registered as the risk, and it settles what the mechanism is about. The MLP does not want token identity in the abstract; it wants its own independently learned map from token to MLP-output space. Sharing the value path's table makes one tensor serve two objectives whose gradients disagree, and it serves neither. So the cheap route is closed and the remaining question is purely one of price on the expensive route. Since the tables must be per-layer and independent, the only axis left is their width. Narrowing each table to 128 dimensions with its own projection up to n_embd keeps every property the mechanism needs, per-layer and independently learned, at 13.4M parameters instead of 50.3M.

**Change.** One mechanism added to champion t0367, the width-reduced form of t0372. A new mlp_embeds ModuleDict holds one nn.Embedding of vocab_size by me_rank per layer with me_rank set to 128, and a me_proj ModuleList holds one bias-free Linear from me_rank to n_embd per layer. GPT.forward gathers and projects per layer, and MLP.forward adds gate * me after c_proj, where gate is 2*sigmoid(me_gate(x[..., :128])) with me_gate zero-initialised so the gate starts at 1.0. The tables join value_embeds_params, inheriting the value-embedding learning rate and the decay at lr*wd = 0.00238 that t0367 established; the projections join matrix_params and so run on Muon. Tables are initialised uniform at plus or minus sqrt(3) and cast to bf16 like the value embeddings, and each projection is initialised uniform at plus or minus sqrt(3/me_rank) so the composed path lands at RMS 1. This adds 13.4M parameters rather than t0372's 50.3M.

**Predicted effect.** Expected between 0.9620 and 0.9650 against champion 0.964001. The throughput term should land near 2 percent rather than t0372's 4.71: the gather moves a quarter as many bytes, the optimizer carries state for 13.4M rather than 50.3M parameters, and the added per-layer projection is arithmetically negligible at about 0.25 percent of step FLOPs. The mechanism is the unknown: at full width it was -0.002296, and if a 128-dimensional per-layer code retains most of that this takes the championship, while if the mechanism scales with width it will land near the champion and say the effect needs the full 512 dimensions to exist, which would close the direction since full width has already been measured as unaffordable.

**Stated risk.** The width reduction here is not the same constraint that failed in t0371, and I want that on the record so a null is not misread: t0371 shared one table across all twelve layers, which destroyed per-layer independence, while this keeps twelve independent tables and only narrows each. Still, both are rank constraints on the same kind of object, and t0371 showed this model tolerates them badly, so a large regression would suggest token-to-space maps here simply need full rank. The second risk is that I am now three trials into one mechanism, having spent them on premise, on a free substitute and on price, which is more than this campaign usually justifies for a single idea; if this does not take the championship I will stop rather than scan the width, because the two endpoints will then bracket the answer well enough to say the mechanism is real and unaffordable, which is a finding rather than a loose end.

## t0375 · explore · keep · val_bpb 0.963254

- **best after:** 0.963254  ← new champion

**Hypothesis (pre-registered).** The MLP token-identity direction is closed and I am honouring the stopping rule I wrote into t0374. Three trials bracket it: own per-layer tables at full width give mechanism -0.002296 but cost 4.71 percent of steps; reusing the value embedding gives +0.000707, worse than nothing, because one tensor cannot serve two paths whose gradients disagree; and narrowing the tables to 128 dimensions destroys the mechanism, giving +0.000421, while barely reducing the cost. That last pair is the useful part. Quarter the parameters cost almost the same tax, +0.002517 against +0.002826, which means the price is not optimizer state but the per-layer full-width gather, gate and add, exactly what the bandwidth model prices near 2.8 percent regardless of table width. So the mechanism is real, needs full width, and this budget cannot afford another per-layer full-width path at any width that preserves it. This trial goes back to the champion's own mechanism with something free. Muon's weight decay is scheduled, get_weight_decay returns WEIGHT_DECAY times (1 - progress), and the training loop applies that only to groups whose kind is muon, so every AdamW group holds a constant decay for the whole run. When t0361 and t0364 set the table rate to MATRIX_LR times WEIGHT_DECAY they matched the matrices' peak and then applied it flat. That is the third distinct way the matching was not a match, after the cautious mask found in t0368, and unlike the first two the fix costs nothing.

**Change.** Two coupled changes on champion t0367, holding the time-averaged decay fixed while changing its shape. First, the optimizer now records initial_wd for every group alongside the existing initial_lr, and the training loop applies group['initial_wd'] * (1 - progress) to any non-muon group whose initial decay is positive, which is exactly the value-embedding group. The shape now matches what the Muon groups have always had. Second, TABLE_DECAY_RATE goes from 0.00238 to 0.00476 so that a linear ramp from peak to zero has a mean of 0.00238, the champion's constant value. The token embedding and unembedding stay at weight_decay 0.0, the scalar groups are untouched, and the Muon groups keep their own schedule unchanged. No architecture change, no new parameters, and no extra work per step beyond one multiply on a scalar, so throughput should match the champion.

**Predicted effect.** Expected between 0.9625 and 0.9650 against champion 0.964001. Steps should be within noise of 2772 since nothing about the per-step work changes, making this the cleanest read in many trials: no throughput term worth subtracting, so the raw number is the mechanism. A decrease says the shape matters and decaying the tables hard early then releasing them matters more than the average rate, which would also explain why the flat 0.00476 of t0365 overshot so badly while its ramped equivalent here does not. A null says only the time-average matters and the schedule is irrelevant for this group, which closes the shape question and leaves the champion's constant decay as correct by accident.

**Stated risk.** The two changes are coupled by design, since holding the mean fixed requires doubling the peak, so a null cannot separate a useless schedule from a schedule whose benefit exactly offsets a peak that is now too high. I considered ramping at the unchanged 0.00238 peak instead, but that halves the average decay and moves along a rate axis whose response I already know is non-monotonic, so that version would be equally confounded and would additionally give up the championship shot. If this lands near the champion the disambiguation is the ramp at 0.00238 peak. The specific mechanism risk is that the value embeddings are a sparse lookup: a ramp concentrates decay early, when rare-token rows have been seen least and their estimates are worst, which is either exactly right, shrinking what is least known, or exactly wrong, erasing rows before they are ever learned.
