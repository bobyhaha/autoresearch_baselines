## t0394 · explore · discard · val_bpb 0.966023

**Hypothesis (pre-registered).** MLP token identity closes, and the way it closes is the finding. Tables on all twelve layers gave -0.002296 of mechanism; on the last four, +0.001273; on the first four, about +0.0015. The whole helps and neither half does, which is strong non-additivity rather than a bad choice of subset -- my two guesses about which layers mattered were both wrong, and in opposite directions, because the premise that some layers carry the mechanism was itself wrong. It is the same shape the value embeddings showed in t0370 and t0371, where sharing or factorising the tables destroyed them: this family of mechanisms is irreducibly per-layer and needs every layer at once. At twelve layers the rent exceeds what it buys, so the complete statement is that the mechanism is real, requires the full stack, and cannot be afforded in a 300-second budget. I said before t0393 that a loss would close this permanently rather than inviting a search over subsets, and it does. This trial follows instead from the one rule this session produced that predicts rather than describes: gating pays for content retrieved from elsewhere and private to the layer. Every gate in this model decides from the query side -- the champion's is 2*sigmoid of a projection of the layer input. But if the mechanism is judging whether what a head fetched is worth emitting, the head is better placed to judge by looking at the fetched vector than at the query that fetched it. The current form must predict usefulness in advance; this form observes it.

**Change.** One change on champion t0379, replacing rather than adding so this is an A/B on what the gate reads rather than a capacity increase. attn_gate becomes an nn.Parameter of shape (n_head, head_dim), and the gate is computed as 2*sigmoid((y * attn_gate).sum(-1)) from the head's own attention output, instead of 2*sigmoid(attn_gate(x[..., :128])) from the layer input. The parameter is zero-initialised so the gate is exactly 1.0 at step 0 and the model starts identical to the champion. Its (4, 128) shape is the same Muon shape group the previous gate occupied, it still lives inside transformer.h, and it still receives a gradient on every step through every layer. Position, granularity and the 2*sigmoid form are all unchanged, as t0355 through t0358 established them. The value-embedding gate is untouched.

**Predicted effect.** Expected between 0.9600 and 0.9635 against champion 0.962349. The throughput term should be near zero: the projection disappears and is replaced by an elementwise multiply and a reduction over head_dim on a tensor already in registers, so if anything this is marginally cheaper, but I will read steps first rather than predict, as four consecutive throughput predictions have been wrong. A decrease says the gate should read what was retrieved rather than what was queried, and would be the first candidate this session derived from a validated rule rather than from reading code or decomposing a result. A null says the two signals carry the same information for this purpose, which is plausible since x is what produced q and therefore what selected y.

**Stated risk.** The honest risk is circularity: y is a weighted average of value vectors selected using q, which came from x, so the content-based signal is not independent of the query-based one and the test may be measuring a reparameterisation rather than a different mechanism. If it lands within noise that is the likeliest explanation, and the informative follow-up would be gating on both jointly rather than either alone, though this campaign has punished added capacity often enough that I would want a clear signal first. A second risk is that a dot product against a single learned direction per head is a weaker readout than a full projection of 128 channels, so a regression could reflect gate capacity rather than the choice of what it reads.
