## t0434 · explore · timeout · val_bpb —

- **best after:** 0.961216

**Hypothesis (pre-registered).** Every head in this model is fully position-sensitive: RoPE rotates all 128 head dimensions. Partial rotary rotates only the first half and passes the tail through untouched, so each head carries both position-sensitive and position-invariant channels rather than being forced to encode content through a rotating basis.

The reason to expect this to matter HERE specifically, rather than as a generic architecture preference, is what the last four trials established about this model's attention. Placement work showed the model depends on periodic global integration and cannot afford a long local-only run: clustering the full-context layers cost +0.0031 and dissolving them into uniform medium spans cost +0.0028. So the four full-context layers are doing content retrieval over 2048 tokens, and at that distance the rotary phase between query and key has wrapped many times at base 30000 -- rotation is close to noise for the long-range matches those layers exist to make. Position-invariant channels give exactly those layers a way to match on content alone, while the eight 256-token layers keep enough rotated channels for local order. That is a specific prediction about a specific structure, not a general preference.

It is also nearly free, and may be slightly cheaper than the current code: half as much elementwise multiply-add in the rotary, though the concatenations may cancel that.

**Change.** apply_rotary_emb is replaced by a partial version governed by a new ROTARY_FRAC = 0.5 constant. The first 32 of each head's 64 rotary pairs are rotated exactly as before; the remaining 32 pass through untouched. No new parameters, no change to shapes, no change to qk-norm, which is applied after this and still sees the whole vector.
Verified numerically on CPU before queueing rather than trusting the algebra: frac=1.0 reproduces the existing function to 1e-6, untouched channels pass through bit-exact, rotated channels match the full rotation on their slice, the rotated block preserves norm under a true cos/sin pair, and no NaNs appear. The edit asserts the current function matches the expected full-rotary form and refuses frac=1.0 as a no-op.

**Predicted effect.** I expect a small effect either way and the step count to be roughly flat -- the arithmetic is halved but two extra concatenations appear, and this campaign has already learned that cost lives at kernel boundaries rather than in operation counts, so I am not predicting a throughput gain. Best case -0.001 to -0.002 if the long-context layers are genuinely handicapped by full rotation; realistic case a null inside the 0.0012 floor; worst case a loss of similar size if the model was using the full rotary basis productively and half the channels is too few for local order in the 256-token layers.
Reading the step count first as usual. If steps move by more than ~0.5% the concatenations are costing real bandwidth and the raw delta needs the throughput correction before it means anything.

**Stated risk.** Crash risk is the main one and it is why this was verified on CPU first -- an indexing error here would burn the coordinate permanently, and slicing cos and sin to the rotated width is exactly the kind of off-by-one that passes ast.parse and fails at runtime. The remaining runtime risk is a shape mismatch under torch.compile with fullgraph=True that the eager CPU test would not surface.
The interpretive risk is that 0.5 is one point on a continuum and a null at 0.5 says nothing about 0.25 or 0.75. If this is null I will not read the axis as closed; the campaign has already made that error three times. And if it wins, the win is confounded between two different mechanisms -- giving long-range layers content-only channels, and giving short-range layers fewer rotated ones -- which a per-layer rotary fraction would be needed to separate.
