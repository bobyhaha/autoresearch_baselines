## t0453 · explore · failed · val_bpb —

- **best after:** 0.961216

**Hypothesis (pre-registered).** Retry after a contention abort, not a redesign. The first attempt was killed by the throughput guard at step 242 while running 145ms per step against a healthy 105ms, MFU 28.8% against 39.8% -- roughly 7.1 steps/s against the guard's 8.0 floor. The training loss was descending normally at the moment it was killed, so the edit is fine and the box was busy. A guard abort still writes a failed record, so the coordinate is burned and this needs a distinct source hash.

The champion's initialiser does not do what its own comment says. The gate-init block reads 'Gate weights init to zero (sigmoid(0)=0.5, scaled by 2 -> 1.0 = neutral)' and then zeroes only ve_gate. attn_gate -- added when the attention output gate was promoted at t0353 -- appears exactly twice in the file, at its definition and its use, and keeps PyTorch's default nn.Linear init.

I measured what that actually does rather than estimating it, and the measurement changed the hypothesis. Feeding rms_norm'd activations through a default-initialised nn.Linear(128, 4) and applying 2*sigmoid gives mean 1.0015, std 0.2628, min 0.155, max 1.828, with the middle 98% spanning 0.425 to 1.575. Crucially the PER-HEAD means are 0.9999, 1.0057, 1.0015 and 0.9990 -- essentially exactly 1.0. So there is no systematic per-head mis-scaling. The scatter is per-token: at initialisation the gate multiplies each head's attention output by input-dependent noise with the right mean and a large spread.

That is a different mechanism from the one I first wrote down. My analytic estimate said 0.72-1.28 and attributed it to relative mis-scaling between neighbouring matrices, by analogy to t0440 and t0443. The real effect is twice as wide and is multiplicative noise, not mis-scaling -- and noise cuts both ways, since it can regularise as easily as it can damage. Zeroing the weight removes it entirely and starts every gate at exactly 1.0, which is what the comment intends.

History still favours testing it: t0046 proposed this gate WITH the zero-init and lost on raw while winning on mechanism; t0353 re-bought the mechanism 300 trials later WITHOUT it and was promoted, so the champion inherited the version missing the line. Three later nodes carried the zeroed form but each bundled it with another change under test, so its own effect is unmeasured.

**Change.** One line inside the existing gate-init loop: torch.nn.init.zeros_(block.attn.attn_gate.weight) alongside the ve_gate zeroing, making the code do what its comment claims. No new parameters, no new tensors, no change to the forward pass.
Notably this run KEEPS max-autotune. Initialisation runs before torch.compile and does not change the traced graph, so the inductor cache still hits and there is no wall-clock risk -- unlike every structural probe since t0439, this is an unhandicapped comparison against the champion on equal throughput footing. I should have realised this at t0443, which reverted compile mode for an init-only change and paid 2.2% of steps for nothing.

**Predicted effect.** Steps should be unchanged -- initialisation runs before torch.compile, the traced graph is identical and the inductor cache hits -- so this is an unhandicapped comparison and the raw delta is the mechanism, judged directly against the 0.00085 floor. A step deviation beyond ~0.5% means contention and voids it.
Now that I know the effect is per-token noise rather than per-head mis-scaling, I am genuinely unsure of the sign and my prior is weaker than when I queued this. Removing initialisation noise should help if the noise is simply damaging early training; it should hurt if the noise is doing useful work, and there is a specific reason it might -- with the weight at exactly zero every head's gate starts identical and receives identical gradients through the gate path, so head symmetry is broken only by the rest of the block. Range -0.002 to +0.002, most likely inside the floor.

**Stated risk.** Implementation risk is minimal: one line in an existing loop, guarded by an exact-string assert and a refusal if attn_gate is already zeroed, dry-run verified.
The substantive risk is the one the measurement exposed. t0353 was validated WITH this noise present, so it is part of what won, and a comment/behaviour mismatch is evidence of an oversight but not of a defect. If this loses, the most likely reading is that the per-token gate noise is a useful regulariser or symmetry-breaker at initialisation, which would be a more interesting result than the fix working -- and it would also mean the same 'unintended default init' framing I used to diagnose t0440 does not transfer here, since there the defect was a genuine scale mismatch and here the mean is already correct.
