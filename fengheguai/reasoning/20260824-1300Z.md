## t0468 · debug · discard · val_bpb 0.964860

**Hypothesis (pre-registered).** Third attempt at depth 13 with width held at 512. The two prior runs were aborted by the throughput guard for starvation -- step 267 at 18.1% MFU and step 369 at 17.5% -- in both cases because a large foreign tenant arrived on the device after the supervisor had selected it. Neither abort said anything about the model, and both logged the intended config: n_layer 13, n_head 4, n_embd 512.

Worth recording that this abort also validated the guard's new instrument in the opposite direction from the last one. The run was at 8.09 steps/s -- ABOVE the old 8.0 step-rate floor -- so the previous guard would have let a badly contaminated run finish and be scored. MFU catches fast-enough-but-starved runs as well as allowing slow-but-efficient ones.

The depth axis was never tested. It was tested as a model-SIZE axis, and nobody noticed -- including me, until t0459.

model_dim is derived from depth: ceil(depth * ASPECT_RATIO / HEAD_DIM) * HEAD_DIM. At the champion's ASPECT_RATIO 42 that gives:
    depth  8 -> width 384, 3 heads
    depth 10 -> width 512, 4 heads
    depth 11 -> width 512, 4 heads
    depth 12 -> width 512, 4 heads   <- champion
    depth 13 -> width 640, 5 heads
So the early exploration of 8/10/11/12/13/14 compared models of three different widths. Depth 12 won because it is the LARGEST depth that still rounds to 512 -- the champion sits at the top of a width plateau by an accident of integer rounding, not because 12 layers was measured against 13 layers. t0459 confirmed the cost of crossing the plateau: depth 13 at aspect 42 is 142.6M parameters against 96.5M, its steps fell 33.9%, and although it gained -0.017064 of capacity -- six times the MLP 5x arm -- it paid +0.024253 in throughput and lost.

Pairing depth 13 with ASPECT_RATIO 39 gives 13*39 = 507, which rounds to 512 with 4 heads: the champion's exact width and head count, one extra layer. This is the first test of depth 13 that is about depth. Within the plateau the evidence points one way -- 10, 11 and 12 are all width 512 and the deepest won -- and this asks whether that trend continues past the rounding boundary that has been hiding it.

**Change.** No functional change: this node already carries DEPTH 13 and ASPECT_RATIO 39 from its parent, giving 13 layers at the champion's width 512 and 4 heads. The edit adds a comment recording the two contention aborts, which makes it a distinct coordinate -- a guard abort writes a failed record and burns the hash.

**Predicted effect.** Parameters rise by roughly the size of one block plus one value-embedding table, so per-step FLOPs rise ~8% for the matrices. There is also a side effect I want on the record: the window pattern 'SSL' tiled over 13 layers puts long-window layers at 2, 5, 8, 11 plus the forced last one at 12 -- FIVE long layers instead of four, so attention FLOPs rise ~20% rather than ~8%. That is part of what 'one more layer' means under this pattern, but it means the arm is not a pure +1 block either.
Net expectation: steps fall to roughly 2500-2600 against the champion's 2806, a token-law debit near +0.0050. The capacity credit is the residual. From t0459, capacity was worth -0.00036 per percent of parameters at the 640-width point; a linear transfer to ~+10% parameters here predicts about -0.0036 and therefore a small loss. But the whole point is that within-plateau depth has behaved differently from cross-plateau size, so I do not trust that transfer and the measurement is the reason to run it.

**Stated risk.** This is a two-constant edit and someone reading the ledger could reasonably call it two changes. I think that is wrong -- ASPECT_RATIO is not an independent knob here, it is the coefficient in the depth->width formula, and any depth change at constant width must move it -- but the objection is fair enough that I am stating it rather than hiding it. The alternative, changing depth alone, is what t0459 did and it measured something else entirely.
The extra long-window layer is the real confound: if this wins, part of the win may be the fifth full-context layer rather than the extra block, and separating them would need a 13-character window pattern holding the long count at four. If it wins I will run that separation before believing the depth story.

This model's shapes are exactly where that should matter: a 512-wide residual stream, a 2048-wide MLP and an 8192-row vocabulary are small enough that curated heuristics are unlikely to contain the best tiling, which was t0058's original argument before it timed out.

Nothing about the model changes, so the evaluation graph stays cached from the champion's own runs -- the failure that killed t0438 does not apply here.

If EXHAUSTIVE finds better tilings I expect +1% to +3% of steps over the champion, worth -0.0006 to -0.0017. If the curated space already contains the best configs for these shapes, steps land at the champion's 2806 and this is a clean null that closes the compile axis.

The durable risk is one I flagged when the champion was promoted and which grows here: cached configurations are tuned against whatever SMs and clocks were free at compile time on a shared box that has produced a 23% slow device and power capping at 697 of 700W. A larger search space means more configurations selected under one machine state and reused under others. If this wins, the confirmation runs are the place staleness would first show, as disagreement between two runs of identical source -- and I would read that as real rather than as noise.
