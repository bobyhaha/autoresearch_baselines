**Hypothesis (pre-registered).** Fourth attempt at a question that is still unanswered. Three prior runs were killed by contention -- two guard aborts near 29% MFU, one at 18.1% -- and none said anything about the edit; each had a normally descending loss when killed. Checking the ledger, the only nodes carrying this zero-init that ever produced a score are t0046, t0363 and t0394, and each bundled it with a different change under test, so its own effect has never been measured in 465 trials.

It is also the cheapest bet available: initialisation runs before torch.compile, so the graph is unchanged, the inductor cache hits, max-autotune is retained and the comparison is unhandicapped -- the shortest exposure window of anything I can queue, which matters on a contended box.

The champion's initialiser does not do what its own comment says. The gate-init block reads 'Gate weights init to zero (sigmoid(0)=0.5, scaled by 2 -> 1.0 = neutral)' and then zeroes only ve_gate. attn_gate -- added when the attention output gate was promoted at t0353 -- appears exactly twice in the file, at its definition and its use, and keeps PyTorch's default nn.Linear init.

I measured what that actually does rather than estimating it, and the measurement changed the hypothesis. Feeding rms_norm'd activations through a default-initialised nn.Linear(128, 4) and applying 2*sigmoid gives mean 1.0015, std 0.2628, min 0.155, max 1.828, with the middle 98% spanning 0.425 to 1.575. Crucially the PER-HEAD means are 0.9999, 1.0057, 1.0015 and 0.9990 -- essentially exactly 1.0. So there is no systematic per-head mis-scaling. The scatter is per-token: at initialisation the gate multiplies each head's attention output by input-dependent noise with the right mean and a large spread.

That is a different mechanism from the one I first wrote down. My analytic estimate said 0.72-1.28 and attributed it to relative mis-scaling between neighbouring matrices, by analogy to t0440 and t0443. The real effect is twice as wide and is multiplicative noise, not mis-scaling -- and noise cuts both ways, since it can regularise as easily as it can damage. Zeroing the weight removes it entirely and starts every gate at exactly 1.0, which is what the comment intends.

History still favours testing it: t0046 proposed this gate WITH the zero-init and lost on raw while winning on mechanism; t0353 re-bought the mechanism 300 trials later WITHOUT it and was promoted, so the champion inherited the version missing the line. Three later nodes carried the zeroed form but each bundled it with another change under test, so its own effect is unmeasured.

**Change.** One line inside the existing gate-init loop: torch.nn.init.zeros_(block.attn.attn_gate.weight) alongside the ve_gate zeroing, making the code do what its comment claims. No new parameters, no new tensors, no change to the forward pass.
Notably this run KEEPS max-autotune. Initialisation runs before torch.compile and does not change the traced graph, so the inductor cache still hits and there is no wall-clock risk -- unlike every structural probe since t0439, this is an unhandicapped comparison against the champion on equal throughput footing. I should have realised this at t0443, which reverted compile mode for an init-only change and paid 2.2% of steps for nothing.

**Predicted effect.** Steps should be unchanged -- initialisation runs before torch.compile, the traced graph is identical and the inductor cache hits -- so this is an unhandicapped comparison and the raw delta is the mechanism, judged directly against the 0.00085 floor. A step deviation beyond ~0.5% means contention and voids it.
Now that I know the effect is per-token noise rather than per-head mis-scaling, I am genuinely unsure of the sign and my prior is weaker than when I queued this. Removing initialisation noise should help if the noise is simply damaging early training; it should hurt if the noise is doing useful work, and there is a specific reason it might -- with the weight at exactly zero every head's gate starts identical and receives identical gradients through the gate path, so head symmetry is broken only by the rest of the block. Range -0.002 to +0.002, most likely inside the floor.

**Stated risk.** Implementation risk is minimal: one line in an existing loop, guarded by an exact-string assert and a refusal if attn_gate is already zeroed, dry-run verified.
The substantive risk is the one the measurement exposed. t0353 was validated WITH this noise present, so it is part of what won, and a comment/behaviour mismatch is evidence of an oversight but not of a defect. If this loses, the most likely reading is that the per-token gate noise is a useful regulariser or symmetry-breaker at initialisation, which would be a more interesting result than the fix working -- and it would also mean the same 'unintended default init' framing I used to diagnose t0440 does not transfer here, since there the defect was a genuine scale mismatch and here the mean is already correct.

**Hypothesis (pre-registered).** Ask whether the aspect ratio itself is mis-set, by moving several steps along the depth-width trade at held compute.

Two brackets were measured this session, both at constant width or constant depth, one step either side of the champion:
    ADDING parameters   width pays about twice depth   -0.000442 vs -0.000209 per % of params
    REMOVING parameters depth punishes harder          3.61x convexity vs 2.3x for width
Those disagree about where a marginal parameter belongs, and one step is too short a lever to settle it -- the model may simply be near a local optimum in both coordinates while the ratio between them is wrong.

depth*width^2 is the leading compute term. Champion: 12*512^2 = 3145728. Depth 8 at width 640: 8*640^2 = 3276800, +4.2%. So four layers are traded for 25% more width at roughly held compute, and the step count should move far less than in any capacity trial so far -- which is what makes this a shape question rather than a size question.

'Shape at constant budget' is this campaign's most productive pattern: it won four times on decay schedules and produced the cleanest negative results on attention placement. This is the same question asked of the one structural ratio nobody has been able to vary independently, because DEPTH silently sets width.

**Change.** Two constants encoding one reallocation: DEPTH 12 -> 8 and ASPECT_RATIO 42 -> 72, giving 8 layers at width 640 with 5 heads. The edit asserts the champion's 12/42 and verifies the rounding arithmetic before writing. Compile mode reverts to default since every GEMM shape in the model changes. Dry-run verified against champion source.

**Predicted effect.** Steps should be close to the champion's, since compute is held to within 4.2% -- that is the signature that distinguishes this from every capacity trial today, where steps moved 7-34%. If steps move much more than ~5% beyond the compile revert's ~2.2%, then depth*width^2 is not capturing the cost and the arm is measuring something other than shape.
On the mechanism I genuinely do not know the sign, which is why it is worth running. The per-parameter argument says width should win. The convexity argument says removing four layers should be punished severely -- 3.61x was measured for ONE layer and this removes four, well outside the range where that number was established. If the convexity dominates, expect a large loss, +0.005 or worse. If the per-parameter argument dominates, this could be the first structural win in a long time.

**Stated risk.** The honest weakness is extrapolation: the 3.61x convexity was measured at one layer either side of 12, and I am applying it four steps away. That is exactly the move that has failed me repeatedly today, and I am making it deliberately rather than accidentally -- the point of the trial is that the short-lever measurements cannot answer a long-lever question, so it must be measured rather than predicted.
Depth 8 was tested early in the campaign and lost, but at width 384, which was a much smaller model rather than a reallocation; that result says nothing about this one.
Secondary: width 640 gives 5 heads instead of 4, so head count moves with width -- the arm bundles a head-count change it cannot separate. And the 'SSL' pattern over 8 layers yields a different long-window count again, so attention structure shifts too. This is the least clean single change of the session, and if it wins the follow-up work is disentangling it, not promoting it blindly.
