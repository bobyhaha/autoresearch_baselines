## t0483 · explore · timeout · val_bpb —

- **best after:** 0.961036

**Hypothesis (pre-registered).** The model casts wte and all twelve value-embedding tables to bfloat16 in init_weights, and leaves lm_head in fp32. No node in 482 trials has ever cast it. That asymmetry looks like an omission rather than a decision: lm_head is the same kind of object as the tables around it -- a large vocabulary-sized matrix, 8192 x 512 -- and it feeds the output matmul, which runs inside an autocast(bfloat16) context, so its weights are converted to bf16 on the way into that matmul on every step regardless. Holding them in bf16 removes the conversion and halves the traffic on the largest single weight read per step.

The campaign's cost model says this should be worth something. Memory traffic, not FLOPs, has set the price of every mechanism measured here -- the attention gate cost 2.8% of steps for a 128x4 matmul because of what it touched. lm_head is 4.2M parameters; in fp32 that is 16.8MB read and converted per step.

It is also the same reasoning the model already applied to its other tables, which is the strongest argument that it is safe: this is not a novel idea, it is the existing idea applied consistently.

**Change.** One line beside the existing wte cast: self.lm_head.to(dtype=torch.bfloat16). No new parameters, no architectural change, no change to the forward pass beyond the dtype the weights are held in. The edit asserts the wte cast is present as an anchor and refuses if lm_head is already cast. Dry-run verified against the current champion source.

**Predicted effect.** Two effects and they point in opposite directions, which is why this needs measuring rather than reasoning.
Throughput should improve: fewer bytes read and no per-step conversion for a 16.8MB matrix. I would guess +0.5% to +2% of steps, worth -0.0003 to -0.0012. Read the step count first as always.
Quality may degrade: lm_head is the output projection, its init std is 0.001 -- deliberately tiny -- and bf16 has 8 bits of mantissa. Weights that small in bf16 have coarse relative resolution, and the AdamW group holding them will now update in bf16 rather than fp32. The wte and value-embedding tables survive this treatment, but they are inputs to a gather, not the matrix that produces logits.
So: a throughput gain and a precision risk, and the sign of the sum is genuinely unknown.

**Stated risk.** The precision concern above is the real one and it has a specific mechanism: lm_head is initialised at std 0.001, and bf16's ~3 decimal digits of precision means updates smaller than roughly 1e-6 relative to the weight will round away. Over 2800 steps that could stall learning in the output layer specifically, which would show as a loss curve that tracks the champion early and separates late.
If it loses, the informative follow-up is not to abandon bf16 but to ask whether the fp32 master-weight behaviour of this optimizer is what the other tables are relying on too -- in which case the existing wte and value-embedding casts may themselves be costing something nobody has measured.
